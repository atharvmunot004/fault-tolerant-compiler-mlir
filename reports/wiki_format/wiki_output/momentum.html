Momentum

Momentum is a conserved vector quantity that captures the ``quantity of motion'' of a system.

Linear Momentum

\[ \vec{p} = m\vec{v} \]

Newton's second law in its most general form:

\[ \vec{F}_{\mathrm{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \]

Conservation of Linear Momentum

When the net external force on a system is zero:

\[ \vec{p}_{\mathrm{total}} = \text{constant} \]

This governs collisions and explosions.

Elastic vs Inelastic Collisions

  • Elastic: Both momentum and kinetic energy conserved
  • Inelastic: Only momentum conserved; kinetic energy lost to heat/deformation
  • Perfectly inelastic: Objects stick together

Impulse

The impulse-momentum theorem:

\[ \vec{J} = \int \vec{F}\, dt = \Delta\vec{p} \]

Angular Momentum

\[ \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p} = I\vec{\omega} \]

Conserved when net torque is zero --- this is why a spinning ice skater speeds up when pulling their arms in.
