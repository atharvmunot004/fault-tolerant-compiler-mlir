Newtonian Mechanics

Newtonian mechanics is the foundation of classical physics, describing the motion of macroscopic objects at everyday speeds. It was formulated by Isaac Newton in his landmark work Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica (1687).

The Three Laws

Everything in classical mechanics flows from three deceptively simple laws:

  • First Law (Inertia): An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force.
  • Second Law (Force): The net force on an object equals the rate of change of its momentum: $F = ma$
  • Third Law (Action-Reaction): For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Core Concepts

Explore the foundational pillars of classical mechanics:

  • Kinematics --- The geometry of motion, without worrying about forces
  • Dynamics --- How forces cause and shape motion
  • Energy & Work --- Energy, work, and the conservation laws
  • Momentum --- Linear and angular momentum

Why It Matters



Newtonian mechanics remains the workhorse of engineering, astronomy, and everyday physics. Its limits --- near light speed or at quantum scales --- led directly to relativity and quantum mechanics.
