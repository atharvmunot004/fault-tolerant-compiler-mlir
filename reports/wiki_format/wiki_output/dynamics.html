Dynamics

Dynamics studies the relationship between forces and motion. While kinematics asks how an object moves, dynamics asks why.

Newton's Second Law in Depth

The central equation of dynamics:

\[ \vec{F}_{\mathrm{net}} = m\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \]

This is actually a differential equation --- solving it given initial conditions and force laws yields the full trajectory.

Common Force Laws



Gravity (Near Earth)



\[ \vec{F}_g = -mg\,\hat{\jmath} \]



Hooke's Law (Spring)



\[ \vec{F}_s = -k\vec{x} \]



Friction



\[ f_k = \mu_k N, \quad f_s \leq \mu_s N \]



Free Body Diagrams



The essential tool: isolate the object, draw every force acting on it, then write $\sum F = ma$ for each direction.



Lagrangian Mechanics



A more powerful reformulation of dynamics using energy rather than forces:



\[ \mathcal{L} = T - V \]



The equations of motion follow from the Lagrangian Mechanics principle of least action.
