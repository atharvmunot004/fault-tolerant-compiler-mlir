Reference Frames

A reference frame is a coordinate system relative to which we measure positions, velocities, and accelerations.

Inertial vs Non-Inertial Frames

  • Inertial frame: Newton's laws hold as-is. Any frame moving at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is also inertial.
  • Non-inertial frame: Accelerating frames where fictitious forces appear (centrifugal, Coriolis, Euler forces).

Galilean Transformations

Between two inertial frames $S$ and $S'$ where $S'$ moves at velocity $V$ relative to $S$:

\[ x' = x - Vt, \quad t' = t \]

\[ v' = v - V \]

Velocities add linearly --- this breaks down near the speed of light (special relativity).

The Rotating Frame

In a frame rotating with angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$, Newton's second law becomes:

\[ m\vec{a}_{\mathrm{rot}} = \vec{F} - 2m\,\vec{\omega}\times\vec{v} - m\,\vec{\omega}\times(\vec{\omega}\times\vec{r}) \]

The extra terms are the Coriolis force and centrifugal force --- they're fictitious but have real effects (hurricanes, Foucault pendulum).
