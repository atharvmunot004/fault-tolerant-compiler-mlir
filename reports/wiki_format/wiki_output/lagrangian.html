Lagrangian Mechanics

Lagrangian mechanics is an elegant reformulation of classical mechanics that replaces forces with energy --- making it vastly more powerful for complex systems.

The Lagrangian

Defined as kinetic minus potential energy:

\[ \mathcal{L}(q, \dot{q}, t) = T - V \]

where $q$ are generalized coordinates --- any coordinates that describe the system's configuration.

The Euler--Lagrange Equations

The equations of motion follow from the principle of stationary action:

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_i} - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = 0 \]

One equation per degree of freedom. For a single particle in 1D, this recovers $F = ma$.

Why It's Powerful

\begin{enumerate} \item Coordinate-free: Choose any convenient coordinates --- polar, spherical, anything \item Constraints are automatic: No need to explicitly handle normal forces \item Symmetry $\to$ Conservation: Noether's theorem connects every symmetry to a conserved quantity \end{enumerate}

Example: Simple Pendulum

Using angle $\theta$ as the generalized coordinate:

\[ \mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2} m \ell^2 \dot{\theta}^2 - mg\ell(1-\cos\theta) \]

The Euler--Lagrange equation gives:

\[ \ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{\ell}\sin\theta = 0 \]
